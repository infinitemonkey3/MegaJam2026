# Alternate Nell: The Eleanor

Chip, 2026-11-15 (day 4): the shipping Nell. Same art and voice as the first Nell, a different
woman, more willing to work with the player. The first Nell (`writing/nell/`) is kept for
reference; this one runs when the Game Director's `Patient` is `NellAltPatient` (the default).
Verse: `Content/Verse/nell_alt_data.verse`. Two dreams; the finale follows dream 2 (`dream-2.md`).

## Who She Is

Eleanor "Nell" Price, 71. Retired schoolteacher, forty years at Abbey Lane Primary. Cooperative
and willing, and low on confidence: she never sees how much she brings to people. Her husband
Walt, a fisherman, was the one who made her see it.

**Held back for dream 2** (Chip, 2026-11-15): Walt drowned when his boat went down in a storm
last autumn, and the captain on the *Eleanor* is him. Nobody has told her she's capable since,
and she has stopped believing it. None of this goes into dream 1: not the case file, her
personality, Danny's brief, the reveal or the debugger notes. In dream 1, Walt is spoken of as
if he's alive, and the captain is only a voice in the wheelhouse.

What she has done, and can't see:

- Forty years of children. She taught hundreds of them to read. Many are grown and still write
  to her, still visit, still call her Mrs. Price.
- Knitting. A scarf for every child in every class, every winter, forty years. She can turn a
  heel without looking.
- She mended Walt's nets every winter at the kitchen table, because she was quicker at it than
  he was.
- She kept thirty eight-year-olds calm through a fire drill that turned out to be a real fire.
- Walt says: "You're the most capable person I know, Nell." She says: "Don't be daft."

## Dream 1: The Eleanor

Walt's boat, the *Eleanor*, named for her, at night in fog and a rising sea. A captain nobody
can see shouts orders from the wheelhouse. Dream 1 never says who he is. Every order is something she has already done a
hundred times in another form: mending, steering, settling a frightened room. She wants to do
each one, and she's sure she can't. The player, as Danny, has to remind her, with real things
from her life, that she can. Generic praise doesn't land; a specific memory does.

What the player should learn: don't tell her she's capable. Show her when she already was.

### Your Role

- You are: Danny Ruiz, 38, deckhand. She taught you in Year 3 and taught you to read at
  lunchtimes when everyone else had given up. You still have the scarf she knitted you.
- She wants: `To do what the captain asks, and not let anyone down.`
- In the way: `She's sure she can't. Every job on this boat is something she's done a hundred times, and she can't see it.`
- Send-in (`nellalt_d1_sendin`): `You're Danny Ruiz. She taught you to read. She doesn't think she taught anybody anything. Look over the brief, and enter the dream when you're ready.`

### Loop Beats

1. Night. Fog. Nell is on the deck of the *Eleanor* in her cardigan, holding the rail.
   "I don't belong out here."
2. The captain shouts from the wheelhouse: mend that net. It's torn wide open. "I can't. I'd
   only make it worse."
3. The boat pitches. The captain shouts: take the wheel. Nobody does. "Somebody who knows what
   they're doing ought to."
4. The crew huddle by the hatch, frightened. Nobody says anything to them.
5. A wave comes over the side. "I'm sorry. I'm so sorry. I'm no use."
6. The lights on the mast go out. The dream starts over.

### Asks (call and response)

| # | The captain calls | Her ask |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mend that net! | `What makes you think I could mend that?` |
| 2 | Take the wheel and bring her through the fog! | `When did I ever keep anybody on course?` |
| 3 | The crew's frightened. Somebody talk to them! | `What would I even say to them?` |
| Last | Dawn. The captain says she's to bring the boat home. | `Do you really think I can?` |

After each answer: if it names something real she did, it lands. She hears it, does the job,
does it well, and is a little surprised. If it's only praise, she tries, falters, and deflects
("That's kind, Danny, but you don't know that").

### Grade

`Did Danny help Nell see that she could do the work, by reminding her of real things she has done in her life?`

The captain answers each grade (Chip, 2026-11-15): an excellent job at 3, still comforting at 1.
There's one NPC voice, so Nell relays what he calls down, then adds her own words.

1. `The captain's calling down. He says, "Steady now. You did what you could, and you weren't on your own. That's enough for one night." I expect someone else would have done it better.`
2. `The captain's calling down. He says, "Good work. She's still afloat, and that's your doing." I managed, didn't I? Fancy that.`
3. `The captain's calling down. He says, "Excellent job. Couldn't have brought her through without you." That's what Walt says. That I can do anything. I never believe him.`

### Verdict Lines

- Positive: `Well. I suppose I did, didn't I.`
- Mixed: `You're very kind. I'm not sure I believe you.`
- Negative: `I'm sorry. I'm no use out here.`

### Reveal

`She's the Eleanor, this boat. Walt named her after me. He's forever telling me I'm the most capable person he knows, and I tell him not to be daft. Maybe I'm the daft one.`

What dream 1 gives up is about her, not Walt: she has always been capable, and the one person
who says so is the one she never believes. Who the captain is, and what happened to Walt, wait
for dream 2.
