## The Dream

The *Eleanor* comes into port at night. The crew are her old students, faces and all, and they
gather round her, cheering. She asks why. They tell her: because she's the new captain. Old
Captain Walt is retiring, and the *Eleanor* is hers now. She turns, and the captain stepping out
of the wheelhouse is her husband. She begs him to stay with her. He holds her and tells her the
journey goes on alone now, but it does go on. She asks how she'll ever find her way without him,
and he points up: the sky over the harbour is full of constellations she's never seen. Each one
has a shape, and she asks him what each one means. What she's looking for is his confidence in
her, and he gives it by tying each constellation to a moment from their life.

What the player should learn here: the same lesson as dream 1, from the one person she's always
heard it from. Don't tell her she can. Show her when she already did.

Walt's brief (the memories the player can tie the stars to; anything he invents is true):

- You met at a dance at the Seamen's Hall. She trod on your feet all night and apologised for every one.
- The first winter you were married, she mended your nets at the kitchen table, faster than you ever could.
- She knitted you a jumper every winter for fifty years. You were wearing one of them in October.
- Forty years at Abbey Lane Primary. Two hundred of her old students came to her retirement do.
- The night of the big storm, your first year married, she kept a lantern in the window till dawn, and you steered home by it.
- You always told her, "You're the most capable person I know, Nell." She always said, "Don't be daft."

## Loop Beats

1. Night. The *Eleanor* comes into harbour. The crew are cheering. "What's all the fuss for?"
2. The crew gather round her. They have faces now: her old students, grown.
3. The captain steps out of the wheelhouse. It's Walt. "Walt? Walt, it's you."
4. He walks down the gangway into the dark without a word. She can't follow.
5. The sky over the harbour is empty. No stars. "I don't know the way home."
6. The harbour lights go out. The dream starts over.
